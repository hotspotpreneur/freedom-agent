# UK Vegetable Growing Guide
## The 5 Easiest Vegetables for Beginners

A UK-specific beginner's guide with accurate soil mixes and timing.

---

## 1. Carrots 🥕

**Soil mix:** 50% sharp sand + 50% stone-free topsoil

**Why:** Carrots hate rich compost — it causes forked, twisted roots. Sand ensures drainage and straight growth.

**When to sow:** April to June (directly in the ground)

**How to grow:**
1. Create a fine, deep seedbed — remove all stones
2. Mix equal parts sharp sand and stone-free soil
3. Sow thinly, 2–3cm apart
4. Thin to 5–8cm apart (don't transplant)
5. Keep moist until germination

**Harvest:** June–October

**Tips:** Stony soil = forked carrots. Heavy clay? Use raised beds with the sand/soil mix.

---

## 2. Tomatoes 🍅

**Soil mix:** 70% peat-free compost + 30% vermiculite

**Why:** Vermiculite improves drainage and retains moisture — prevents blossom end rot.

**When to sow:**
- Indoors: March–April
- Plant out: Late May (after last frost)

**How to grow:**
1. Start seeds indoors on a warm windowsill
2. Pot on as they grow
3. Use 70/30 compost + vermiculite mix in containers or grow bags
4. Water consistently — inconsistent watering causes blossom end rot
5. Feed weekly once flowers appear
6. Pinch out side shoots (cordon varieties)

**Best UK varieties:**
- Outdoor: 'Gardener's Delight', 'Sungold' (cherry types)
- Patio: 'Tumbling Tom', 'Balcony'

**Harvest:** July–September

**Tips:** Cherry tomatoes are most reliable outdoors. Consistent watering is key!

---

## 3. Kale 🥬

**Soil mix:** 60% compost + 40% soil

**Why:** Kale is the easiest — thrives in any soil, including clay. Not fussy at all.

**When to sow:**
- Spring sow: March to May
- Winter sow: August (for winter harvests)

**How to grow:**
1. Sow indoors or directly outside
2. Transplant or thin to 45cm apart
3. Keep well watered in dry spells
4. Harvest outer leaves, let the centre keep growing

**Best UK varieties:**
- 'Curly Scots' — frost-hardy
- ' Nero di Toscana' (Tuscan kale) — italian type
- 'Redbor' — purple, ornamental

**Harvest:** October–February

**Tips:** Frost makes kale sweeter! This is a winter veg that actually benefits from cold weather.

---

## 4. Runner Beans 🫘

**Soil mix:** 70% compost + 30% well-rotted manure

**Why:** Beans are nitrogen-fixers but love rich soil. Manure boosts fertility.

**When to sow:**
- Indoors: May
- Direct sow: After last frost (late May)

**How to grow:**
1. Soak seeds overnight before sowing
2. Create wigwam supports (3–4 canes tied at top)
3. Sow 2 seeds per cane, thin to 1
4. Plant at base of supports
5. Water well, especially once flowering begins

**Harvest:** July–September

**Tips:** Pick regularly — the more you pick, the more the plant produces. Leave pods too long and the plant stops cropping.

---

## 5. Lettuce 🥗

**Soil mix:** 60% compost + 20% coco coir + 20% perlite

**Why:** Coir and perlite retain moisture while perlite ensures drainage. Lettuce won't bolt as easily.

**When to sow:** March to August (succession every 3 weeks)

**How to grow:**
1. Sow directly or in modules
2. Thin to 15cm apart
3. Keep soil consistently moist
4. Harvest outer leaves (cut-and-come-again)

**Best UK varieties:**
- 'Little Gem' — compact, sweet
- 'Salad Bowl' — cut-and-come-again
- 'Lollo Rossa' — frilly, colourful

**Harvest:** May–October

**Tips:** Succession sow every 3 weeks for constant supply. Lettuce tolerates partial shade.

## UK Frost Dates

- **Last frost:** Most of UK = late May
- **First frost:** Most of UK = October/November
- Don't plant tender crops (tomatoes, beans) outside before late May

---

## General Tips

- **Start small:** 2–3 vegetables > 10 vegetables you can't tend
- **Water consistently:** Most crop failures = uneven watering
- **Mulch:** Helps retain moisture, reduces weeds
- **Pests:** Watch for slugs, aphids, cabbage white caterpillars
- **Harvest regularly:** The more you pick, the more you grow!
